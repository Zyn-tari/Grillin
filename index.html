Grilling the Plan

Turning a vague ask into an executable plan that survives contact with reality — by attacking every claim in it, including your own, before anyone builds anything.

The core rule. Adversarial effort spent before building is worth many times the same effort spent after. Three of the eleven phases produce no plan text at all. They only produce corrections. They are the highest-value phases.

Operating the plan — after it turns out to be wrong

The eleven phases are written for the author, before anything runs. On the first run measured end to end, the plan-maker/operator separation held about twenty minutes. Five gaps reported separately were one gap with five faces: there was no execution-time method.

How to build a reader

The only part of the method backed by a number. The gate caught 2 defects. The readers caught 50. Two roles, and they are not the same role — on that run the health checker disqualified itself from the adversarial pass, correctly, having read the plan by round two. The thing that makes it good at its job makes it useless at the other one.

The checkable half is ownership: the adversary's owner must appear nowhere else in the plan. Caveats on the number, stated rather than buried: one job, one operator, one domain; the two layers catch disjoint classes and are not the same kind of count; the gate ran continuously and the readers in bursts, so this is yield, not rate. Nothing in this repository runs the gate — no hook, no CI — so every rule above is enforceable, not enforced.

Two phases never scale down, at any project size: grill the documents against the running system, and verify the environment the plan assumes. They are the cheapest phases and they catch the most expensive errors.
